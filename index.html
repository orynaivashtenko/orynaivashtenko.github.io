Oryna Ivashtenko

Ph.D. candidate in astrophysics, Weizmann Institute of Science

I am a Ph.D. candidate in physics at the Weizmann Institute of Science, working with Barak Zackay. I develop statistical methods for detecting weak astrophysical signals in complex noise, and for turning such marginal detections into calibrated population-level conclusions.

My main project is an independent search for small, long-period planets in the full Kepler data set. The pipeline uses a detection statistic that accounts for correlations and non-Gaussianity of the noise and for physical priors. It calibrates the probability of planetary origin for every candidate using per-target permutation and injection tests. The goal is a catalog of planet candidates near the detection limit, each with a well-defined probability of being real, and an occurrence rate of small long-period planets that propagates these probabilities and the candidates’ radius posteriors into the population inference.

I have also worked on gravitational-wave data analysis, searching for precessing binary black holes. Many of the statistical techniques are shared between gravitational waves and planet detection, and I am excited to keep exploring their applications in new fields, especially Gaia astrometry.

Before Weizmann, I studied applied physics at V. N. Karazin Kharkiv National University in Ukraine.

Scientific interests

I am currently interested in detection and statistical population studies of exoplanets, binary stars, and compact objects. I like luminous things that orbit one another and leave signatures in the data. I like to detect them and count them in a sophisticated way, to then infer physics from the counts. In general, I like various elegant statistical problems that help us understand physics.

Experience

Time-series analysis (mainly through Kepler, a bit of TESS and LIGO), a bit of image analysis (mainly through Kepler difference imaging), a bit of astrometry (Gaia), occasional exposure to pulsar astronomy.
