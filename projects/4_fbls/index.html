fBLS, a fast-folding transit search

A fast-folding BLS algorithm designed for ultra-short-period planets whose parameter space is too large to search in the classical way.

fBLS is a fast-folding technique for transit searches, built on the fast-folding algorithm, a dynamic-programming method that is widely used in pulsar astronomy. For a light curve with N points, it produces the binned phase-folded light curves for an array of Np trial periods, and then computes the standard BLS periodogram and statistics for each. The cost is O(Np log Np) operations instead of the O(Np N) of a regular BLS, which matters most for planets with periods shorter than a day, where the period grid is dense.

A preliminary fBLS search for ultra-short-period planets in the Kepler main-sequence light curves, followed by a simple signal-validation scheme, recovered all known ultra-short-period candidates and found three new ones (Shahaf et al., 2022). This work, led by Sahar Shahaf with Barak Zackay, Tsevi Mazeh, and Simchon Faigler, dates from my M.Sc. years at Weizmann.

References

  1. fBLS – a fast-folding BLS algorithm
    S. Shahaf, B. Zackay, T. Mazeh, S. Faigler, and O. Ivashtenko
    Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society 513, 2732–2746, 2022
    abstract · arXiv · journal · bib

    We present fBLS – a novel fast-folding technique to search for transiting planets, based on the fast-folding algorithm (FFA), which is extensively used in pulsar astronomy. For a given light curve with N data points, fBLS simultaneously produces all the binned phase-folded light curves for an array of N_p trial periods. For each folded light curve produced by fBLS, the algorithm generates the standard BLS periodogram and statistics. The number of performed arithmetic operations is O(N_p log N_p), while regular BLS requires O(N_p N) operations. fBLS can be used to detect small rocky transiting planets, with periods shorter than one day, a period range for which the computation is extensive. We demonstrate the capabilities of the new algorithm by performing a preliminary fBLS search for planets with ultra-short periods in the Kepler main-sequence light curves. In addition, we developed a simplistic signal validation scheme for vetting the planet candidates. This two-stage preliminary search identified all-known ultra-short planet candidates and found three new ones.
