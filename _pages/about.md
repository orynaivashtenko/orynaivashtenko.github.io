I am a Ph.D. candidate in physics at the Weizmann Institute of Science, working with Barak Zackay. I develop statistical methods for detecting weak astrophysical signals in complex, non-Gaussian noise, and for turning such marginal detections into calibrated population-level conclusions.

My main project is an independent search for small, long-period planets in the full Kepler data set. The pipeline uses a detection statistic that accounts for the non-Gaussianity of the noise and for physical priors, and it calibrates the false-alarm probability of every candidate with permutation and injection tests. The goal is a catalog of planet candidates near the detection limit, each with a well-defined probability of being real, and an occurrence rate of small long-period planets that propagates these probabilities and the candidates' radius posteriors into the population inference.

I also work on gravitational-wave data analysis with the same philosophy: sampler-free parameter estimation for compact binary coalescences, and searches for precessing binary black holes using mode-by-mode filtering and marginalization.

Before Weizmann, I studied applied physics at V. N. Karazin Kharkiv National University in Ukraine. Outside research, I have taught statistics and experimental design to graduate students, and I have coached and judged teams at the Ukrainian and International Physicists' Tournaments.

A full [CV](/cv/) and the list of [publications](/publications/) are on this site; my papers are also listed on [ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/search/q=author%3A%22Ivashtenko%2C%20O%22&sort=date%20desc%2C%20bibcode%20desc) and [ORCID](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-2805-9405).
